# real estate as an asset, 2026-10-14 (Spain first, then global)

## headline

- **Spain's housing boom is still accelerating, not rolling over.** INE IPV Q2 2026: **+12.2% y/y** (new +7.4%, second-hand +12.9%) and **+3.4% q/q**; the notarial appraisal index (MIVAU *valor tasado*) set a **nominal all-time record of 2,355 €/m², +12.5% y/y** — but is still **~22.5% below its 2008 real peak** ([INE](https://www.ine.es/dyngs/INEbase/es/operacion.htm?c=Estadistica_C&cid=1254736152838&idp=1254735976607), [CaixaBank Research 2026-09-17](https://www.caixabankresearch.com/sites/default/files/content/file/2026/09/17/34454/2026_09_17_nb_espana_pvm_2t2026.pdf)).
- **Nominal 2007 peak is long gone; real 2007 peak is not.** Registradores' repeat-sales index is **+39.31% above the Q3 2007 peak** and **+127.68% above the end-2014 trough**; the Bank of Spain's CPI-deflated series was still **−14.7% vs the Q3 2007 peak** at end-2025 ([Registradores ERI 2T2026](https://www.registradores.org/documents/d/guest/eri_2t_2026), [BdE-IEF]).
- **The supply gap is the whole story.** 12-month new-build permits to Feb 2026: **141,135**; 2025 completions: **~92,000**; net household formation in the 12m to Q2 2026: **239,000**. Bank of Spain estimates a **cumulative deficit of ~755,000 homes since 2021**, potentially **>1m by 2028** ([CBR], [BdE, via EjePrime 2026-09-23](https://www.ejeprime.com/residencial/el-banco-de-espana-pide-politicas-de-oferta-eficaces-ante-el-aumento-de-precios-de-la-vivienda)).
- **Money is tightening, not easing, on the Spanish mortgage side.** Euríbor 12m closed September 2026 at **3.251%** (highest of the year, +0.47pp in six months; Oct provisional mean 3.217%); new mortgage rates **3.03%** (Q2), average new mortgage **€176,453** (record), monthly payment **34.3% of wage cost** — accessibility at its worst in ~15 years ([Euribor.com.es / BdE](https://www.euribor.com.es/2026/09/26/euribor-de-septiembre-2026-el-banco-de-espana-certifica-el-3251-y-marca-el-mes-mas-caro-del-ano-para-las-hipotecas-variables/), [Registradores ERI 2T2026](https://www.registradores.org/documents/d/guest/eri_2t_2026)).
- **The 2026 reform attacks the rental and speculative margin, not the price level.** RDL 26/2026 (BOE-A-2026-20266) blocks corporate purchases below **70% of appraisal value** until 31/12/2028 and imposes a **25% special SOCIMI charge** on undistributed residential-rental profits; RDL 27/2026 (BOE-A-2026-20385) makes 5/7-year **successive mandatory renewals** the default and forces **≥12 months' rent** compensation on a landlord who declines to renew. Neither measure adds a single dwelling.

### Spain — RDL 27/2026 (BOE-A-2026-20385, in force 02/10/2026)

Source for all rows: BOE text in vault, `.dsh-research/vivienda/rdl27-2026.txt`; [BOE-A-2026-20385](https://www.boe.es/boe/dias/2026/10/01/pdfs/BOE-A-2026-20385.pdf).

| article | measure (label: all FACT, source [BOE27]) |
|---|---|
| art. 10.1 LAU | On expiry of the term or any extension, after ≥5 years (7 if the landlord is a legal person), if neither party gives notice — **landlord 6 months, tenant 2 months** — the lease is **mandatorily extended in successive 5-year (7-year) periods**. The old tacit annual renewal capped at 3 years disappears: continuity is the rule, termination an express act |
| art. 10.1 ¶2-¶4 | A landlord who validly refuses to renew must **compensate the tenant with ≥12 monthly rents** for an equivalent dwelling (where possible on the state rent-reference system), **never less than 1 month's rent per year resided**, payable on handover; **sale does not extinguish the right** (the subrogated buyer owes it); no compensation where the tenant qualifies for a mandatory legal extension, even if unrequested |
| art. 10.2 | Five written, itemised grounds removing compensation: (a) natural-person landlord needs the home for self/second-degree relatives/spouse (compensation revives if unoccupied after 3 months); (b) tenant did not live there >6 of the last 12 months without justification; (c) tenant holds another suitable dwelling in the municipality; (d) the parties sign a new lease; (e) tenant rejects a firm offer guaranteeing ≥5/7 years and rent set under art. 17.6 LAU, even outside a tensioned zone; (f) accredited landlord vulnerability |
| art. 10.3-10.6 | Voluntary extensions after a refusal cannot be shorter than 5/7 years and clauses worsening the tenant's position are **null**; **extraordinary 1-year extension on accredited vulnerability**, mandatory for a *gran tenedor*; **extraordinary 3-year extension in a tensioned zone** (mandatory), mutually exclusive with and preferential to other statutory extensions |
| DA 1ª / DT única | Coordination with RDL 26/2026's 2-year extraordinary extension; applies to existing contracts **only for expiries after entry into force**; contracts already in the old art. 10.1 extension period run it out under the old wording; those in *tácita reconducción* move to the new regime at the first expiry after 4 months, counting total elapsed time; **notices already validly served keep their effect and generate no compensation** |

## bull

- **Nominal scarcity is real and documented.** 12-month permits (141,135) and 2025 completions (~92,000) sit far below net household formation (225,000 residents + 55,000 non-resident purchases in 2025; 239,000 households in the 12m to Q2 2026). The Bank of Spain's ~755,000-unit cumulative deficit, with >1m possible by 2028, is a supply-side argument, not a sentiment one.
- **Transacted prices are still compounding, with breadth.** 80% of municipalities above 25,000 inhabitants rose more than 10% y/y and — for the first time in the series — **no municipality fell**. Both official-ish indices (INE +12.2%, MIVAU tasado +12.5%, Registradores repeat-sales +16.7%) point the same way.
- **Real prices in the two main crisis markets are still below prior peaks in real terms.** Spain's tasado value is 22.5% below its 2008 real peak and its CPI-deflated IPV roughly 12-15% below the 2007 peak; US Case-Shiller y/y is only +1.9%. If the bull case is "catch-up to a prior real peak", there is arithmetic room in Spain and none in the US.
- **Demand is not credit-fuelled in the same way as 2004-2007.** 26.5% of buyers pay cash and new-build is only ~21% of sales (vs ~40% in 2004-2007); the Bank of Spain explicitly says the current risk configuration differs from the pre-GFC one and lending standards are "prudent". Leverage-driven distress is contained: *daciones en pago* at a series low (306).
- **The reform can be read as institutionalising scarcity rents for incumbents.** Blocking sub-70%-of-appraisal corporate purchases, capping rent updates, taxing vacant and tourist units and penalising non-renewal reduce the optionality of holding housing as a trading asset — which mechanically reduces turnover and therefore observed supply.

## bear

- **The asset has already priced the shortage.** Spain's price-to-income ratio is 153.9 (2015=100) vs an OECD average of 132.2 and a US 151.9; price-to-rent is 116.7. The Registradores index is 39.31% above the 2007 peak. Anything bought at 2026 prices is buying a 12-year compounding trend at its maximum extension.
- **Financing costs have turned against the buyer, and the margin is thin.** Euríbor 12m went 2.221% (Feb) → 3.251% (Sept), new mortgage rates 3.03%, US 30-year at 7.40%, US 10-year at 5.24%. The Spanish monthly instalment already absorbs 34.3% of the wage cost (55.8% in the Balearics), and the Bank of Spain reports ~38% of income going to housing — the worst in 15 years.
- **Demand is already cracking while prices have not.** Registered sales fell 5.7% q/q to 167,934, the fewest in seven quarters; the trailing 12-month count grew only 1% (from 5.2% in Q1 2026); mortgage registrations fell 3.3% q/q. Registradores itself frames this as "demand is not insensitive to price".
- **The 2026 decrees increase legal cost and reduce exit options without adding supply.** A ≥12-month indemnity on non-renewal, mandatory successive 5/7-year renewals, a 25% SOCIMI charge on undistributed residential rental profits, IBI surcharges up to 150% on tourist units, VAT on short lets and the 2% rent-update cap all reduce the risk-adjusted return of the marginal landlord while leaving construction bottlenecks (land, licensing, labour) untouched. Sector bodies (idealista, CNC, APCEspaña) read it as an offer withdrawal.
- **China shows what the endgame of a leveraged housing cycle looks like, and it is becoming disinflationary rather than inflationary.** Development investment −19.9% y/y, new-home space sold −12.1%, new starts −24.8%, new-home prices −3.0% y/y, household loans down CNY 1.03trn in eight months and mortgage balances still contracting, despite 3.1% mortgage rates, a 1pp state interest subsidy, lead-bank/project-company rules and completed-homes-only sales. Nine-figure developer restructurings (Country Garden, Evergrande) are still being negotiated in 2026.
- **The "AI deflation" channel cuts against residential real estate as a scarcity asset.** Data centres are outbidding housing for land and construction labour, and remote work was quantified as having *raised* house prices by ~15pp in 2020-21 — a demand shock that is fading or reversing, not compounding. Meanwhile offsite/automated construction, if it ever lands, is a deflationary supply shock to replacement cost.
- **US housing is the counterexample to the "shortage means rising prices" thesis.** With a ~1.2m-unit shortage, Case-Shiller y/y is +1.9%, existing-home sales are 3.99m SAAR and affordability, per a Fed governor, is at a 21-year low. Scarce supply plus a 7.4% mortgage can mean frozen, falling-volume markets rather than rising prices.
