## headline

- **The carry trade is bigger than in 2024 and now costs money to run.** Jefferies: cross-border yen borrowing **+67% to ~¥360tn (~$2.3tn)**, Dec 2021→Mar 2026 — "the largest carry-trade cycle of the past three decades"; BoJ at **~1.25%** `[S1][S6]`.
- **Japan is still the top foreign holder of US Treasuries — and is selling.** **$1,103.9bn (2026-07)** vs **$1,239.3bn (Feb 2026)** and **$1,155.4bn (2025-07)**; foreign official T-bills **−$103bn** in three months `[S2]`.
- **5 Aug 2024 unwind: Nikkei −12.4% (−4,451.28 pts, worst since Oct 1987), VIX to 65.73 intraday (record) closing 38.57, USDJPY 142.96 (+2.5% d/d, +14% in under a month), BTC −15% intraday below $50k** `[S3][S4][S20][S13]`.
- **The long end is the 2026 story.** US 10y **5.24%**/30y **5.60%** `[S7]`; JGB 10y **3.115%** (30-yr high), 30y **4.235%** (record) `[S8][S10]`; Bund **3.65%** (17-yr high), BTP-Bund **109.9bp** `[S11][S12]`; **TLT −54.5%** from its Jul-2020 peak `[S14]`.
- **Fiscal dominance is priced, not debated.** FY2026 US deficit **$2.0tn** (CBO final), debt held by public **$32.4tn**, ACM 10y term premium **0.885%** (Sep 2026) vs **−1.377%** (Jul 2020) — while the Fed *hiked* into the oil shock on 16 Sep 2026 `[S15][S16][S17][S18]`.

## bull

**Bonds.** (1) Duration now pays: 10y TIPS real **2.91%**, 30y **3.32%**, vs **−1.08%** in Aug 2020 (~4pp swing) `[S34][S43]`. (2) Term premium positive but modest: ACM 10y **0.885%** vs a **2.235%** median since 1961 and **5.205%** in 1984; mean-reverting *down* leaves room `[S17]`. (3) Auctions clear: the 40y JGB drew **3.1 bid-to-cover** (highest since Jul 2020); the 10y saw "robust demand" after hitting 3% `[S10]`. (4) No accident priced: HY OAS **3.11%**, VIX **15.2**; Goldman upgraded both equities and 10y govvies on "improving return prospects for sovereign debt" `[S45][S46][S69]`. (5) The hard-asset bid (gold **$4,164**, record CB buying) is a substitute store of value, not proof of bond-market collapse `[S48][S63]`.

**Carry trade survives.** (1) Differential still wide, yen still cheap: USDJPY **158.4**, Fed **3.75–4.00%** vs BoJ **~1.25%** `[S7][S6][S13]`. (2) Japan has a structural reason to tolerate it: debt **>200% of GDP**, BoJ owns **>half** of JGBs, taper debate "leans slightly dovish", FY2027 assumes only **3.8%** `[S26][S29]`. (3) The 2026 episode did **not** cascade — BTC −3% then recovered, Asia mixed — because intervention, unlike a hike, does not raise funding costs `[S13]`.

## bear

**Bonds / fiscal dominance.** (1) The bear market has not ended: TLT is **−54.5%** from its Jul-2020 peak and made its **trough in Sep 2026**, *after* the Fed hiked `[S14]`. (2) Arithmetic deteriorating into higher rates: FY2026 deficit **$2.0tn** ($140bn above CBO's own baseline), public debt **$32.4tn** (**+$2.4tn** y/y), interest reported to exceed defence *and* Medicare `[S15][S16][S32]`. (3) The Fed is trapped by a supply shock, not free to cut: it hiked to **3.75–4.00%** with inflation "elevated" and Brent **$125/bbl** while buying bills as "reserve management" — the sovereign needs low rates, the CB raises them, so adjustment lands on the long end and the currency `[S18][S50]`. (4) Foreign official front-end demand is retreating: official T-bills **−$103bn** (Apr→Jul 2026); Japan alone **−$135bn** Feb→Jul `[S2]`. (5) Japan is the pressure valve and it is straining: reserves fell a **record $79.6bn in a month** to **$1.208tn** funding intervention, while it runs the biggest carry book in three decades, defends the yen, and finances a record **¥143tn** request at an assumed **3.8%** `[S24][S1][S29]`. (6) Europe confirms it: Bund **3.65%** (17-yr high) *in a fiscally sound country*, BTP-Bund **109.9bp**, ECB hiking into **3.8%** inflation, and **€838.2bn** more German borrowing in 2027–30 `[S11][S12]`.

**The Austrian lens — strongest mainstream critiques.** (1) The deflation tail has a primary-source doctrine: Bernanke (2002) argues that under fiat money a determined central bank "can always generate higher spending and hence positive inflation", citing US CPI **−10.3% (1932) → +3.4% (1934)** after Roosevelt's devaluation `[S57]`. (2) The 2009–2021 record falsified the strongest Austrian predictions: post-2008 QE produced no US consumer-price hyperinflation for a decade; 2021–22 inflation came with fiscal transfers and supply shocks, then fell back before the 2026 oil shock `[S65][S66]`. (3) ABCT is hard to falsify — the standard empirical test (Lester & Wolff 2013) exists because its predictions are hard to distinguish from ordinary cycles; I retrieved the record, not the findings `[S54]`. (4) The 2026 AI-capex-as-malinvestment story has a rival explanation: UBS itself frames the same facts (capex **$940bn** in 2026, off-balance-sheet obligations **~$3.5tn**) as a *credit-structure* problem with identifiable counterparties, guarantees and maturity mismatches — as much private-credit opacity as interest rates `[S58]`. (5) Fairly, the Austrians were right where it counted: negative real yields (**−1.08%**, Aug 2020), credit-financed duration expansion, a **−54.5%** drawdown in long Treasuries, and record CB gold buying at **$4,164** — an observable, not a prophecy `[S34][S14][S63]`.
